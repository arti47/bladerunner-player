> In the throng, you spy your target. It's time to move, Blade Runner.

You are a BLADE RUNNER - detective, pursuer, killer. In a grim, neon-noir future, you investigate cases involving Replicants and other regulated technologies. Using the BLADE RUNNER RPG Core Rulebook - and the tools and guidelines contained herein - you can play as an independent investigator without the need of a Game Runner.

## YOUR SOLO BLADE RUNNER

Create your lone Blade Runner using the standard character creation rules. You do not gain any special benefits or suffer any limitations, and are free to craft your character as best fits your vision. Refer to the guidance below for key aspects of your solo character.

### BLADE RUNNER ORIGIN
| D12 | RESULT |
|---|---|
| 1 | In the wake of a traumatic incident, your dreams invade your waking life. You're not sure what's real anymore. |
| 2 | Your vice has gotten the better of you before. To others, you're a liability. |
| 3 | Your methods are unorthodox and reckless, but you always get results. |
| 4 | You had a partner, but the case went bad. They lost their life, and you lost a friend. |
| 5 | You reported a crooked cop. Others don't trust you. |
| 6 | You've got a knack for finding trouble, and a reputation for collateral damage. |
| 7 | An ongoing investigation into recent actions makes you the department pariah. |
| 8 | After a traumatic childhood, you struggled all your life to make connections. |
| 9 | You returned from off-world. Earth has changed, and so have you. |
| 10 | A desperate gunfight left civilians bleeding in the street. You took the blame. |
| 11 | Amid a sensational case, you drew too much media attention. |
| 12 | You're part of the old guard, and your methods seem dated to young Blade Runners. |

* **ARCHETYPE:** Select any archetype reflecting your character's background and specialty. Or, if you prefer, you may create your character without using any archetype; in this case, choose your key attributes and skills freely, and begin with D8 Chinyen Points.
* **HUMAN OR REPLICANT:** Your Blade Runner can be human or Replicant. Through the course of your campaign, you can reveal that you are a secret Replicant if it is dramatically appropriate, or you might interpret a twist in the investigation as uncovering your true identity. If this happens, follow the guidelines on page 28 of the Core Rulebook to adjust your Health and Resolve scores.
* **YEARS ON THE FORCE:** Since you must rely on a broad set of skills as a lone Blade Runner, seasoned or higher is recommended for a human. All Replicant Blade Runners are rookies. Set your attributes, skills, specialties, Promotion Points, and Chinyen Points as shown on page 28 of the Core Rulebook.
* **SKILLS:** Equip your lone Blade Runner with skills that enable them to independently pursue investigations. OBSERVATION, CONNECTIONS, MANIPULATION, and INSIGHT are particularly helpful.
* **SPECIALTIES:** Any specialties are valid for a lone Blade Runner, but those which benefit other characters (such as Bodyguard or Counselor) are less useful outside of a standard group of investigators.
* **BACKGROUND ELEMENTS:** To solidify your vision for the character, consider how your key memory, key relationship, and signature item help define your history and personality. In addition, use the Blade Runner Origin table to identify what it is about your background or nature that sets you apart and has you working alone.

## PROMOTION AND HUMANITY

As with standard play, your character evolves through Promotion Points and Humanity Points. For your solo campaign, use the checklists below to manage your rewards.

**SESSION MILESTONES:** One of the advantages of solo play is that you aren't limited to the usual structure and timing of group play. In one session you might spend a few minutes playing out a single Shift; in the next, you spend several hours pursuing and solving an entire case. This flexibility can impact the flow of session-based rewards. To make it more consistent, structure your gameplay into major milestones. After around two or three hours of play, when you make a dramatic break in a case or finish a key scene, take a moment to work through the Humanity and Promotion Point checklists. Two or three milestones per case is about right.

### PROMOTION POINT CHECKLISTS

**GAINING PROMOTION POINTS:** Once per session (or milestone), earn one Promotion Point for each of the accomplishments below.
* Found one or more clues that led to real progress in the investigation.
* Revealed one or more fugitive Nexus-8 Replicants and reported them to the RDU.
* Apprehended one or more suspects or fugitives.
* Stopped one or more serious crimes from being committed.
* Uploaded at least one piece of important evidence to the LAPD Mainframe.
* Saved the life of another law officer or performed some other heroic action.
* Reported one or more law officers for some form of misconduct and presented evidence to back up the claim.
* Took a Baseline Test and passed (Replicants only).

**LOSING PROMOTION POINTS:** During a session, immediately lose a Promotion Point when you:
* Fail to apprehend a wanted fugitive when given the opportunity to do so.
* Abuse your authority or act in a way unbecoming of a law officer.
* Ignore or directly violate an order.
* Lose LAPD equipment assigned to you.
* Harm or kill an innocent suspect or bystander.
* Allow the case to receive negative media exposure or stir public controversy.
* Expose confidential case information to the media.
* Accuse Wallace Corp of anything (with or without evidence).
* Fail a Baseline Test (Replicants only).

**CLEARED CASE REWARD:** In addition to the Promotion Point rewards listed above, solo investigators gain bonus Promotion Points at the end of a successful case. See page 012 for details.

**DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS:** As detailed in the Core Rulebook, you must make a CONNECTIONS roll each time you lose a Promotion Point due to misconduct. If you fail, Deputy Chief Holden will hand out a punishment. Imagine an appropriate disciplinary action or choose from the list in the Core Rulebook (page 167). Then, consider how a disciplinary action complicates the case moving forward.

### HUMANITY POINTS CHECKLIST

Once per session (or milestone), earn one Humanity Point for each of the actions you performed from the list below.
* Took a personal risk to help another person - human or Replicant
* Connected to someone, human or Replicant, on a personal level
* Learned something significant about someone's life
* Tried to talk someone down from committing a crime
* Learned something that made them view the world differently
* Refused to upload a piece of evidence to the LAPD Mainframe in order to protect someone
* Refused to follow orders because your character felt they were wrong
* Used your key memory or interacted with it in some way
* Interacted with your key relationship
* Played one or more scenes of Downtime
* Failed a Baseline Test (Replicants only)

## WORKING THE CASE

As with standard play, the core structure of the game is to solve Case Files by visiting locations, finding clues, and establishing leads. This section details the procedures and tools you have at your disposal in a solo campaign.

### STARTING A CASE FILE
As a member of the RDU, you investigate cases involving Replicants and other regulated technologies. To generate the basic facts of a case at the start of the investigation, you have several options:
* **TRUST YOUR GUT:** You can simply decide the nature of the investigation based on what is interesting or a good fit for the BLADE RUNNER universe.
* **FOLLOW A THREAD:** You are often left with unresolved mysteries, unanswered questions, and unintended consequences from a previous case. If these issues warrant a new investigation, it's a perfect opportunity to create a coherent, connected story.
* **USE THE CASE FILE GENERATOR:** Use Case Table 1 (Theme) and Case Table 2 (Assignment) in the Core Rulebook (starting on page 222) to generate the broad details of an investigation. You can answer questions and flesh out the details of a case using other Case File Generator tables in the Core Rulebook, such as Case Table 3 (Main NPCs) and Case Table 4 (Sector).
* **SEEK INSPIRATION:** Use the Case File tables starting on page 014 to inspire a case framework. The Case Briefing tables (page 016) help build the broad overview of a case, and you can use other tools such as the Character tables and Location tables to add detail.

**KEEP IT SIMPLE:** Whatever approach you use for generating the details of the case, don't introduce too much information. There are far more questions than answers at this stage, especially from your character's perspective. Imagine what you would learn in the context of a briefing and let the investigation spin out from there.

**MAKE IT MATTER:** Case Files should involve more than a random crime. They are opportunities to explore the themes of the BLADE RUNNER universe, and to test your character's morality and humanity. When setting the framework for a case, consider how it evokes a dramatic world of conflict and corruption. If a case feels mundane at the start, you can use complications through the course of the investigation to add complexity and depth. This is one of the essential characteristics of noir storytelling - a seemingly-straightforward situation that unravels into shadowy conspiracies and ethical dilemmas.
You should also consider how a case connects to your character's background, personal challenges, and moral codes. For inspiration, use the Personal Hook table on page 017 to learn how this case is meaningful for your character at the start of the investigation. Then, as the investigation proceeds, consider how new revelations put pressure on your character's resolve and integrity.

### GET BRIEFED
Each investigation begins with a briefing in which you are brought up to speed on the known facts of the case. This is usually held by RDU Deputy Chief Holden, but might be presented by a member of the LAPD or by someone further up the UN chain of command - particularly if the case is sensitive.
* **IMAGINE THE BRIEFING:** Take a moment to set the scene, imagining how it plays out. How are you summoned? Where does it take place? What is the attitude of the person giving the briefing? A typical briefing is held at the RDU offices, but a high-profile incident might involve a hasty introduction at the scene - putting you on the case without delay.
* **ASK QUESTIONS:** During the briefing, ask yes or no questions as your character using the simple Question Check (page 009), and complex questions by interpreting responses from the Cipher tables (page 015). But leave unanswered questions. The person conducting the brief doesn't have all the details, nor endless patience. The point of investigating a case is to fill in those blanks.

### TAKE YOUR FIRST STEPS
Working from the limited information you have at hand, decide how to begin your investigation. This likely involves visiting a location - such as a crime scene - to conduct a careful examination or question witnesses, but you might decide to begin with research such as accessing the LAPD mainframe, or consulting with an expert in the crime lab. The next steps are naturally different for a cold case as opposed to one in which potential clues are still fresh.

### YOUR CASE LOG
To ensure your investigation and sessions stay on track, keep a Case Log. Use whatever format works best for you - digital or handwritten - at whatever level of detail you prefer. The Case Log should include notes for clues, leads, hypotheses, persons-of-interest, and locations visited. For deeper immersion, make a written entry as your character to summarize your findings each Shift, or even record in-character voice memos to supplement your notes.

When you finish a session, make sure your Case Log is up to date with the latest details. At the start of the next session, review your entries to get back up to speed.

### INVESTIGATION PROCEDURE
The summary below provides an overview of an investigation in solo play. Check the sections that follow for details on these terms and procedures.
1. Proceed to a location (page 006).
2. Make a Countdown Event Check (page 006) to see if you encounter an interruption or complication.
3. At the location, play out one or more scenes (page 006). Optionally, at the start of a scene or situation, make a Scene Check (page 008) to help set the complexity and danger.
4. Make skill rolls (page 009) to overcome challenging and risky circumstances. Gather clues (page 011) and reveal details in each scene using Question Checks (page 009) and Case File Tables (page 014).
5. Once all scenes at a location are resolved, consider the information you gained, update your Case Log, and review your Hypotheses (page 012).
6. When you take an action or encounter a circumstance which will conclusively prove or disprove a hypothesis, make a Hypothesis Check (page 012).
7. If a Hypothesis Check leads to the end of the case, wrap things up and gain Promotion Points per the summary on page 003. Otherwise, the Shift is complete; return to step 1.

**MANAGING SHIFTS:** As in standard play, there are four Shifts in a day: Morning, Day, Evening, and Night. Normally, it is the Game Runner's job to keep track of time. In solo play, a Shift usually passes for each location you visit. In unusual circumstances, you might need an extra Shift to complete a complex activity or a large number of scenes at a location. Do whatever seems sensible.

**WORKING MULTIPLE CASES:** Blade Runners don't always have the luxury of working a single case. Narrative events might inspire you to add another case to your character's workload, or your investigation can uncover a seemingly unrelated cold case. If so, you'll need to manage your time and focus between both investigations. As you dig in, look for opportunities to reveal how the cases are entangled in interesting and surprising ways.

## LOCATIONS

Nothing gets solved sitting at your desk. To investigate a case, you visit locations to find clues, conduct research, meet NPCs, and learn more about potential suspects. As a Shift begins, decide which location you'll visit. This choice is primarily driven by the leads you wish to pursue, although unexpected events may force you to divert to an unplanned location.

* **LOCATIONS AND TIME:** Visiting a location takes a Shift of time. However, what comprises a location is flexible. If you visit an apartment complex and begin with a scene on the street outside the building, the street should not count as its own location. Use whatever is reasonable to set the scope of a location.
* **TRAVEL BETWEEN LOCATIONS:** You can use your Detective Special Spinner to reach any area of Los Angeles within minutes. Unless there is unusual risk or time pressure, this doesn't require a skill roll. Take a moment to imagine the world beyond the Spinner's windows as you soar through the neon canyons and pervasive rain. This is a perfect time to wrap up the previous Shift with an entry in your Case Log.
* **LOCATION INTERRUPTIONS:** In the life of a Blade Runner, even the most routine moments can bring unexpected events. When setting off to a new location, make a Countdown Event Check (below) to see if your plans are undone by an interruption.

### COUNTDOWN EVENTS
To keep the pressure on, a Countdown Event Timer ticks away as you go about your investigation. When this timer triggers, imagine an interruption or misfortune that forces your character to change plans, deal with new information, or overcome an unexpected complication.

* **SETTING THE TIMER:** The Countdown Event Timer is tracked using Base Dice, starting with a single D6. Make note of the timer in your Case Log, or set aside a die to indicate the current value.
* **MAKING A COUNTDOWN EVENT CHECK:** Once per Shift, when you proceed to a new location, make a roll using the current timer.
  * If any timer die rolls one or more successes, a Countdown Event is triggered.
  * If an event doesn't trigger, future events are now more likely. Upgrade the timer to the next Base Die type: D8, D10, D12. If you upgrade a D12 timer, add a second die starting at D6 (giving the timer a rating of D12/D6). For future timer checks, upgrade the second die when an event fails to trigger. The maximum possible timer is D12/D12.
* **INTRODUCING AN EVENT:** A Countdown Event is your opportunity to heighten the drama by throwing a wrench in the works of your Blade Runner's investigation. When a Countdown Event triggers, you can introduce it as an interruption en route to your next location (perhaps forcing a change of plans), or imagine it as something that occurs at the location.
* **GETTING INSPIRATION:** To help inspire the nature of an event, use the Countdown Event table on page 023. This table offers broad categories of events that you can interpret as best fits the situation and case. Get more detail on an event by following up with Question Checks (page 009), or through interpretive prompts such as the Cipher tables (page 015).
* **RESETTING THE TIMER:** After a Countdown Event triggers, reset the timer to its starting value: a single D6. You are back in control for now.

## SCENES

Within a location, play out one or more scenes to progress your investigation. Each scene usually involves a specific activity, such as searching a room, questioning a witness, or staking out a suspect's apartment. For example, if you arrive at a murder scene, you might question first responders, carefully examine the body, and canvass the area for witnesses. Each of those activities is a separate scene - three scenes in total - all taking place at a single location.

Within a scene, make skill rolls (page 009) to overcome risky and uncertain situations. You can use a Scene Check to guide whether a situation requires a skill roll, and reference the Scene Categories table for skill roll examples. This table does not include the entirety of potential scenes and challenges, but covers typical activities for an investigation.

**GENERATING A RANDOM SCENE:** If your investigation hits a wall, you can imagine an outside event or new information - for example, a call from Deputy Chief Holden - that sets you back on a path. If so, roll a D12 on the table below to select a scene category. Imagine the direction or information that motivates that scene, and the location you must visit.

### SCENE CHECKS
In standard play, the Game Runner decides the challenge of a situation. Is the action possible? Is a skill roll required? Is an advantage or disadvantage in play? In solo mode, these decisions are up to you. If you are unsure of the complexity and risk involved when your Blade Runner faces a new scene or situation, make a Scene Check to guide your options and approach.

**MAKING A SCENE CHECK:** Roll a D8 Base Die and check the table below. Rolling a success indicates a positive situation from your character's perspective, while a lower roll is a negative situation. Rolling a 1 or an 8 adds an additional complication or favorable twist, respectively.
* **IT'S TOUGH OUT THERE:** The odds favor a challenging situation. This is true to the BLADE RUNNER universe.
* **NO DEAD ENDS:** Don't let the outcome of the Scene Check block your way forward, or introduce a premature resolution to the case.
* **MAKE IT RELATIVE:** The intensity of a scene will help guide the severity of a Complicated result, or the opportunity presented by Favorable.
* **KEEP IT MOVING:** Scene Checks are a tool, not a requirement. Only make a Scene Check when you aren't sure of the nature of a situation.

| D8 ROLL | RESULT | DETAIL | EXAMPLES |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complicated | On top of one or more skill rolls, overcoming the situation may require some combination of extra time, different approaches, more resources, additional risk, or rolling with a disadvantage | A witness is unexpectedly hostile or attempts to flee the scene; you are blocked from examining a crime scene by corporate agents; the data you seek is confidential, and requires in-person access at the LAPD Mainframe |
| 2-5 | Challenging | Requires one or more skill rolls to proceed | The witness is reluctant to answer your questions, or their answers are evasive; the crime scene is a mess, and requires a careful and lengthy search; finding the data you seek can be done remotely, but requires a TECH roll |
| 6-7 | Routine | Probably does not require a skill roll | The witness is cooperative; there are obvious clues at the crime scene; the data you seek is easily retrieved |
| 8 | Favorable | Probably does not require a skill roll - if one is required, it is made with an advantage | The witness has unexpected insight; a clue at the crime scene provides a promising break in the case; the data you seek connects to some other aspect of the case in a surprising way |

### QUESTION CHECKS
The Question Check functions similarly to the Scene Check, but is used for basic questions that can be answered with a yes or no. These questions can be ones that your Blade Runner is posing to another person within the scene, or ones that you are asking out-of-character to clarify some aspect of a scene or situation.

* **POSING THE QUESTION:** If possible, ask a question so that the "yes" response is the most interesting and dramatic result.
* **MAKING THE QUESTION CHECK:** Roll a D10 Base Die. A 1 or a 10 indicates an extreme response - the yes or no is more definitive than expected, or adds a twist.
* **ADJUSTING THE ODDS:** If a "yes" response has a high probability, roll two Base Dice and discard the lowest. If a "yes" has a low probability, roll two Base Dice and discard the highest.
* **THIS OR THAT:** You can also use a Question Check to determine which of two things are true.

| D10 | RESULT |
|---|---|
| 1 | Extreme no |
| 2-5 | No |
| 6-9 | Yes |
| 10 | Extreme yes |

### SKILL ROLLS
Whether you're a rookie or an old-timer, you're a competent investigator, so the basics of doing your job are not usually left to chance. Thus, you should make a skill roll only when necessary - when the action is risky, uncertain, or complicated.

* **IMAGINE YOUR ACTION:** Take the time to imagine the scene and how you approach the challenge.
* **MANAGING FAILURE:** If you fail an action, imagine what goes wrong. Failure should not stop your story - there must be a way forward, perhaps at a cost.
* **DRAMATIC FAILURES:** The appearance of a 1 on any Base Die indicates a dramatic outcome, heightened consequence, or surprising turn of events.
* **CRITICAL SUCCESS:** Outside of combat, you can apply an appropriate narrative benefit for a critical success, or choose or roll an outcome from the Critical Success table.

| 3 | **Unnoticed:** Perform the action with quiet or subtlety. | If making a STEALTH roll during this scene, gain an advantage. |

In other situations, simply decide the outcome of an NPC's action as best fits the situation, or use a Question Check. Do not push rolls for NPCs. ### GATHERING CLUES
Most investigation scenes involve looking for clues - searching a location, examining physical evidence, interviewing persons of interest, consulting with experts, tracking the movements of suspects, or sifting through data archives.
* **FINDING CLUES:** Depending on the scene and situation, finding a new clue often requires a success on a skill roll.
* **COMPLEX SITUATIONS:** You can't always reveal a clue with a single skill roll. You likely need to overcome several obstacles and dangers first.
* **IMAGINING CLUES:** For inspiration to the nature of clues, use the Case File tables (starting on page 014).
* **ASKING QUESTIONS:** Finding a clue often opens up new questions. Pose questions and answer them using your instincts and random prompts.
* **KEEPING TRACK:** Make note of clues in your Case Log. Refer back to these notes occasionally to look for new connections.

### FOLLOWING LEADS
Leads are directions to pursue based on clues - suspects, locations, or lines of inquiry. They represent where the clues point, guiding you to the next location. If you are floundering, feel free to introduce information from an outside source that gives you a new lead.

### HYPOTHESES
Through the course of your investigation, your character gathers clues and develops leads. From these, you create your hypotheses - theories to the solution of an investigation. Each hypothesis is assigned a dice rating representing its apparent likelihood.

* **HYPOTHESES REVIEWS:** At the end of each Shift, consider the impact of new information on your hypotheses.
  * To add a new hypothesis, give it a D6 rating.
  * To upgrade a hypothesis, move it to the next step: D8, D10, D12. If upgrading past D12, add a second die starting at D6 (up to D12/D12).
  * To downgrade a hypothesis, step it down by one.
* **MINOR HYPOTHESES:** You can also assign a hypothesis to some minor aspect of the investigation, introducing it with an upgraded starting value.
* **ONCE PER SHIFT:** You can add, upgrade, or downgrade any number of hypotheses, but limit the review to once per Shift.

#### HYPOTHESIS CHECK
Eventually, your investigation leads to a dramatic moment in which you test a theory by making a Hypothesis Check. The check should come at a moment when the investigation, story, and the likelihood of a hypothesis have converged.
To make a Hypothesis Check, roll one or two Base Dice per its rating. This roll cannot be pushed.
* **SUCCESS (Gain 3 Promotion Points):** A single success means your hypothesis proves out, but with a mitigating circumstance or twist.
* **CRITICAL SUCCESS (Gain 5 Promotion Points):** If you roll two or more successes, you can tie up this investigation in a neat little bow.
* **FAILURE (Lose 3 Promotion Points):** If you roll no successes, the hypothesis is off the mark. You were mistaken, duped, or betrayed.
* **CONVINCING OTHERS:** You can make a Hypothesis Check when convincing someone of the validity of a hypothesis (e.g., selling your Reporting Officer on the theory). This doesn't prove it or give points, but success gives an advantage to the MANIPULATION or CONNECTIONS roll.

### DOWNTIME
You can't work the streets 24/7. As in standard play, you need a Shift of Downtime after three Shifts of investigation. Otherwise, you start to suffer stress. Use the Downtime Event tables to help imagine how you spend your time.

## COMBAT & CHASES

When playing solo, use the standard rules for combat and chases (as detailed in the Core Rulebook). Included here are special considerations and tools for the solo player.

### ZONES
When entering into combat, first envision your surroundings and plot out your zones. To keep the combat manageable, imagine two or three zones.
* **ZONE FEATURES:** For each zone, determine an identifying feature or roll one randomly using the Location tables. Take note of each zone, tracking your character and any enemies or allies.

### NPC ACTIONS
Without a Game Runner, it falls to the solo player to manage NPCs and direct their actions in combat and chases.
* **DETERMINING NPC ACTIONS:** Consider the enemies involved, their motives, whether they're the aggressor or merely defending themselves, and the environment around them. Use Question Checks to decide specific actions.
* **WHAT'S THE GOAL?** It's rare to engage in a combat scene with an ultimate goal of "defeat all enemies," so consider whether the NPCs involved wish to kill or capture your player character, secure an important item, or accomplish some other critical task.

### RECOVERING FROM BROKEN
As in regular play, if you're Broken and no one is around to help you, you automatically recover one point of Health after one Shift has passed.
* **PATCHING YOURSELF UP:** At the beginning of your turn, if you are Broken, you have one chance to patch yourself up: Make a MEDICAL AID skill roll (with advantage if you have emergency medical gear with you). If you're successful, you immediately regain Health equal to the number of successes in the skill roll.

### CHASES
When pursuing foes or fleeing from them as a solo player, the chase rules function similarly to standard play, with a few minor modifications. The procedure each Round:
1. Determine whether you are prey or pursuer.
2. Choose a chase maneuver.
3. Choose or roll a chase maneuver for the NPCs.
4. Choose or roll an obstacle.
5. Resolve maneuvers, with prey going first.

**GENERATING OBSTACLES:** You can simply imagine the obstacles or use the Obstacle tables in the Core Rulebook.
**MULTIPLE PARTICIPANTS:** The slowest person/vehicle on each side performs maneuvers and rolls dice.
**NPC MANEUVERS:** You can randomly determine NPC maneuvers during the chase using the NPC Chase Maneuvers table.

|---|---|---|

| 2-5 | Pursue | Flee |
| 6-7 | Cut off | Block or hide |
| 8 | Stand and shoot | Stand and shoot |
